## What the report must do

- Answer the architect's six questions in this order, plainly: how problematic the current architecture is; which mechanisms add unnecessary complexity; which capabilities are genuinely needed; what a simpler coherent architecture could look like; what prevents moving towards it; how to get there in steps while the product keeps working.
- Keep the necessity of a capability apart from the necessity of its present implementation (R14). Say where several generations of a solution run at once.
- Keep four things visibly apart: intended target, current implementation, transitional arrangements (each with its end), legacy to retire (R17).
- Present the recommended architecture with its alternatives at the main forks. Say what the two candidates agreed on independently, what the challenge found, and what remains conditional on a decision. Do not present a conditional choice as settled.
- Give the dispositions as a table: capability or mechanism, disposition, one-line reason, confidence, the decision it waits on. Show where assessor, steelman and challenge disagree.
- Present each breaking change as a trade-off: who and what is affected, migration options, cost (R16). User data and capabilities are not disposable.
- Give the roadmap as steps with what each deletes, and name what can be deleted or tried now without a ruling.
- Give the outstanding decisions in the order of how much each unblocks, with the default assumed.
- Say what this implies for Wayfinder, as derived requirements (R19), briefly.
- Say plainly how much to trust it: evidence is representative, not exhaustive; which claims were checked against code by the challenger and with what result; what was read statically and never run; sizes that are estimates; what was not assessed. Counts are pointers, not evidence of unnecessary complexity (R15), and low use in Spec Kitty's own repositories is not evidence that something is unnecessary.
- No history of who caused what (R2). No implementation or remediation is authorised; the roadmap is a proposal. Every number and statement traces to a file in A; cut what cannot be traced. Source references to code may be quoted where they make a pattern concrete (three or four vivid examples serve the reader better than a list).
- Length: body at most 6,500 words; tables and appendix in addition. Plain, short sentences. Explain each technical term once. Use the ecosystem's terms; the domain object is a Mission.
