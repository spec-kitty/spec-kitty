## What this is about

The question is not whether dependency rules are obeyed. It is whether the design is coherent and whether each mechanism deserves to exist. Look for: the same domain rule or responsibility implemented in several places; competing mechanisms for one problem; special cases that bypass an established abstraction or extension mechanism; complexity out of proportion to the need; remnants of an older design that was never fully extracted or retired; mechanisms that no longer earn their keep. The two leading questions: does the code need this complexity, and is this a remnant.

Nothing existing is a fixed constraint (ruling R7): not on-disk formats, not existing Missions, not public APIs, not upgrade paths (R6), not recorded decisions (R1). The target is the best possible 4.0. Judge necessity before implementation quality (R3). Prefer deletion to reorganisation where the need does not justify the mechanism (R10).

Needs come from documented needs and user journeys (user docs, README, command prompts, the charter's stated purpose), not from how often something is used in Spec Kitty's own repositories, which are skewed to the software-dev mission type (R5). Do not over-fit to that usage, in either direction: a rarely used capability may be needed, and a heavily used mechanism may be accidental.

Do not investigate who caused anything or reconstruct history (R2). Use history only to tell a remnant from a living design, or to name a migration constraint.

## Evidence standard

- Every factual statement about the implementation carries a source reference: `repo:path:line` (or a symbol name with path), checked by you at head. Tag it [read], [executed] (you ran something; keep the command) or [inferred].
- Representative evidence is enough to establish a pattern: give three to six concrete, well-chosen examples and a rough measure of spread (a count from a stated grep or query). Do not enumerate exhaustively.
- Scale the evidence to the reversibility of what you recommend. Anything that touches persistent data or a public contract: name the consumers and the data affected.
- Separate uncertainty that would change a decision from uncertainty that an implementation experiment can settle safely.
