# Brief: mechanism assessment (one assessor per mechanism)

Read A/dispatch/COMMON.txt and A/00-mandate-and-rulings.md first. Your mechanism, id, profile and starting points are in your instructions.

Assess necessity before quality. Write A/mechanisms/<id>.md, at most 3,000 words, in this order:

1. **Verdict in five lines.** Provisional disposition (retain, simplify, consolidate, replace, remove, investigate further), confidence (high, medium, low), and the one-sentence reason.
2. **The need.** The user or operational need behind the mechanism, taken from documented needs and user journeys. Who needs it, when, and what goes wrong without it. If you cannot find a documented need, say so.
3. **Essential complexity.** What any honest solution to that need must handle, stated without reference to the present implementation. Keep it short and concrete.
4. **Current mechanisms.** What exists today to meet the need: the modules, commands, on-disk state, configuration, and their rough size (files, lines, number of states, modes, flags, refusal codes, where countable). Name every mode or variant (for example topologies) and what distinguishes it.
5. **Accidental complexity.** Where the implementation exceeds the essential complexity, with representative examples: special cases, modes that exist for one caller, guards that compensate for another mechanism's design, state kept in two places, rules restated in several modules, remnants of a superseded design (say what marks it as a remnant), compatibility paths, and indirection with one implementation behind it.
6. **Duplication and distribution of responsibility.** The same rule or decision made in more than one place; responsibilities split across unrelated locations; competing sources of authority. Give paths.
7. **Is it still necessary?** Mechanism by mechanism and mode by mode: needed as is, needed in a simpler form, not needed. Say what the mechanism compensates for; a mechanism that exists only to protect against another mechanism is a candidate for joint removal.
8. **Simpler alternatives.** Two or three, including the most radical one you can defend (for example: remove it, or delegate it to git, the host tool or the operating environment). For each: what it gives up, what it deletes (rough lines and concepts), what it needs from other mechanisms.
9. **Blast radius.** What consolidation, replacement or removal would touch: callers, commands, on-disk data, public contracts, other repositories, tests, documentation and prompts. Name consumers for anything public or persistent.
10. **Obstructions.** The concrete arrangements that make the simpler form hard to reach today.
11. **Uncertainty.** What would change the disposition (decision-material), and what an implementation experiment can safely settle. Suggest the smallest reversible experiment.
12. **Evidence table.** As A/mechanisms/<id>-evidence.csv: claim, reference (repo:path:line), tag (read, executed, inferred).

You assess one mechanism, but mechanisms interlock: where yours exists because of another one, say so plainly and name it. Do not read other assessors' files in A/mechanisms/.
