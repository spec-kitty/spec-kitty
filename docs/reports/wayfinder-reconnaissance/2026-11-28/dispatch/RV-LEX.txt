# Review brief: wording, terms and claim strength

Read R/dispatch/REVIEW-COMMON.txt first. Profile: lexical-larry. Reviewer id: L.

You review the wording of the documents under R/synthesis/ (every .md directly in that folder) and of a seeded sample of finding claims. You do not re-run measurements.

1. Terms. Check every ecosystem term against the glossary in /home/user/spec-kitty/docs/context/ and against how the five repositories use it. List: a term used in two senses; an overloaded word used without its sense (primary, merge, routing, residue, projection, runner, coordination, charter, pack, service, contract, independence); an outside term (from the design brief's vocabulary) used as if it were an ecosystem term; the same thing under two names across documents; "feature" where the domain object is a Mission.
2. Claim strength. For each document, list sentences whose wording is stronger than the cited findings allow: causal verbs on correlational evidence; universal quantifiers on samples; "verified" on desk assessment; "violation" where a proposal, not a recorded decision, is the reference; "missing" where no recorded intent places the thing; trend wording across the convergence. Open the cited finding and its claim to judge.
3. Consistency. Numbers, revision ids, finding ids and verdict words that differ between documents for the same thing. Finding ids that do not exist. Counts that do not match their denominators.
4. Views. Statements where the view label (recorded-intent, observed, approved-plan, proposed) is missing or does not match the cited evidence kind.
5. Sample of claims. Take the rows of R/review/sample.csv for workstream A2 excluded, and from the remainder those selected_as = decisive: for each, does the claim sentence in R/findings/<WS>/<id>.yaml say exactly what its evidence entries can carry, with scope and denominator?

Outputs:
- R/review/wording-review.md (at most 2,500 words), items graded major (a reader would take away something the evidence does not support), minor, note.
- R/review/wording-items.csv: id, file, location, quoted_text, problem, grade, suggested_reading (what the evidence does allow one to say; you do not rewrite the document).
